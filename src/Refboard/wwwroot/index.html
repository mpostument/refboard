How to use Refboard

Timed sessions

Pick one or more packs (or "All" / a random pool), an interval - a preset, a custom number of seconds, or a structured warm-up/quick/long-study schedule (or build your own under Custom: any number of steps, each its own seconds-per-pose and pose count, saved for next time) - and any options: grayscale, random mirroring, a grid overlay (rule of thirds, golden ratio, diagonals, or just a center cross), a ghost of the previous pose, a focal-point marker, a bell when the pose changes, fullscreen. Tone filtering (high-contrast / low-key / high-key) and skipping near-duplicates are both computed from your own library, not a fixed guess. Click Start drawing.

During a session

Space pause/resume · ← → previous/next pose · F flip horizontally · G grayscale · Q squint · S skip this pose for good · Esc stop. The HUD's own buttons do the same thing for a mouse.

Tonal value / Notan study

The HUD's value select (or V to cycle) posterizes the reference to 2-6 flat tones, shown side by side with the original - block in light and dark masses before chasing detail. Two tones is a pure Notan (light mass vs. dark mass); more tones reads closer to a full value scale. For a quicker check without leaving the plain original, Squint (Q) just blurs it directly - the fastest version of holding a reference at arm's length and unfocusing your eyes to see its big shapes and values.

Understand the pose

Open the Info drawer (I, next to the value select) for everything about the current pose rather than the session itself:

  • Scheme - swap the tonal-value view's colour: grayscale, three duotones (Van Dyke brown, Payne's grey, sepia/burnt umber), a false-colour heatmap, or edge detection.
  • Blur - softens the reference before posterizing, so tone blocks in as soft masses instead of hard edges around every small bit of local contrast.
  • A legend showing exactly what each tone level maps to in the current scheme, a brightness histogram, and the pose's top-5 dominant colours.

Eyedropper (E, its own HUD button) - click anywhere on the reference to read the exact hex colour and brightness percent under the cursor. The last few samples stay listed as small swatches next to the reading - click one to copy its hex, to compare two earlier spots (skin vs. background, say) without re-aiming at either one again.

Two more, both Options-screen checkboxes since they apply to every pose in the session rather than being switched pose to pose: Ghost previous pose overlays a faint edge-detected silhouette of whatever was on screen just before this one, to help track how a gesture or form carries from pose to pose; Focal-point marker marks this pose's own visual-weight centroid - a rough guide to where the eye tends to land first, from its actual brightness distribution rather than a guess.

Zoom and pan

Scroll the wheel, or use the HUD's − / 100% / + (also -/+/0 on the keyboard), to zoom into a detail - a hand, a face - up to 6x. Once zoomed in, drag anywhere to pan; double-click, or the 100% button, resets. Zoom and pan reset automatically on every new pose.

Angle tool

Toggle it with the HUD's Angle button (or A), then drag anywhere on the reference the way you'd hold a pencil up to check a shoulder or hip line before committing to it on paper. The first drag after turning it on also becomes a reference length - drawn faint and dashed once a second line exists - and every drag after that reads its own angle and its length as a ratio of that reference (a head height, a hand span). A few of these comparison lines stay on screen at once (the oldest quietly makes way for a new one past that), so you can measure a shoulder line and a hip line and read both together instead of one at a time. Clear lines, or turning the tool off and back on, wipes the slate for a fresh reference. High-contrast overlays (Options, or H) swaps the colour/opacity used to tell lines apart for a black-outlined white line with its own dash pattern per line - the grid overlay gets the same outline, more visible in bright light either way.

Construction guides

The HUD's guide select (C cycles) offers three more traditional construction aids, one at a time:

  • Vanishing point - drag (following the cursor live) to drop a point and fan guide lines out from it across the whole stage, for perspective construction on a box, a room, or a foreshortened limb.
  • Plumb line - drop a full vertical+horizontal crosshair through a point, the digital equivalent of holding a pencil straight up to check whether one landmark sits directly over another.
  • Proportion divider - drag out one unit the same way the angle tool's own reference length works, and the tool marks off repeating multiples of it automatically along the same line - "how many head-heights tall," measured instead of eyeballed.

Only one guide is ever active at a time, and switching to a guide turns the angle tool and eyedropper off (and vice versa) - all three want the same drag gesture for different things. Selecting "Off," or switching guides, clears whatever was drawn.

Check your own image

The drop zone on the setup screen works with no library at all - it's the whole of what runs on the live demo. Drop one image to view it full-screen with every tool above, paused with no timer running; drop several to run a real timed session against them. Nothing is ever uploaded anywhere - it stays local to this browser.

Rescan library

Shown on the setup screen once a library index has loaded. Click it to ask the container to reindex right away instead of waiting for its next scheduled pass - useful right after dropping a new pack into the mounted folder.
