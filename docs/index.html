            How to use Refboard

            The three things Refboard does

            References - your library of pictures, browsed as a grid and drawn from in timed sessions. Generate - references made to order by your own ComfyUI (only when one is connected). Colours - palettes, with how to mix each colour from your own paints, and the Colour studio. 3D forms is a small extra for practising light on simple solids. The dashboard shows the same groups as cards.

            Find a tool - Ctrl+K, or the magnifier at the top of the rail: type a few letters of what you want and Enter. The moon is the theme, the brush is My materials, the question mark is this page.

            References: the library

            Mount a folder of pictures (see the README) and its packs are listed in the Library panel. Clicking a pack or folder opens it as a grid; its checkbox puts it in the pool a timed session draws from. They are separate on purpose: browsing a pack to decide whether you want it is how you end up ticking it.

            The search box matches file, folder and pack names; the box beside it sets the order - the folder's own, by name, lightest or darkest first, or shuffled. Arrow keys move between thumbnails, Enter opens one, S skips it. A skipped picture is never drawn again but stays in the grid, dimmed, so you can undo it.

            Clicking a thumbnail opens it in the same viewer a session uses, with no clock; Esc returns to the grid. Draw these starts a timed session from exactly what the grid shows. Museums searches the Met and the Rijksmuseum. Check an image opens any picture you drop - a reference, or a photo of your drawing.

            References: a timed session

            Tick packs, then set the right-hand panel: an interval (a preset, a custom number of seconds, or a warm-up/quick/long-study schedule), the tone filter, and options - grayscale, random mirroring, a grid overlay, a ghost of the previous pose, a bell, fullscreen. Then Start drawing.

            Space pause · ← → previous/next · F flip · G grayscale · Q squint · V value split · E eyedropper · A angle tool · D compare your drawing · I info · S skip for good · Esc stop. Zoom is + − 0. The toolbar holds the tools you pin; the rest are under More (the three dots).

            Value split turns the picture into 2-6 flat tones next to the original: block in light and dark masses before detail. Pencil grades says which grade of pencil each area wants. Info (I) shows the histogram, the dominant colours and the scheme and blur of the value view.

            Eyedropper reads the exact colour under the cursor and several ways to mix it from real paint, in your medium. My materials (the brush) says what you draw and paint with, so the value steps and recipes speak in its terms. Compare lays a photo of your drawing over the reference.

            Generate

            With COMFY_URL set, Gen AI makes anime references by choices - a character, a landscape, buildings, nature or an animal, in the light, setting and medium you pick - with a picture or a palette as the starting point, a sketch pad, line art to trace, details enlarged, and a series, the best of several, or colours held to your own palette. What it makes is kept under Uploads and History, ready to open in the viewer.

            Colours

            Palettes makes a palette of colours in harmony and spread over the values, each with how to mix it from your paints; it can also read one from a picture, and send it to Generate. Colour studio has four tabs: Picture (a picture's palette and gamut mask), Wheel (a harmony and the paints that mix it), Mixing (every paint with every other) and Pigments (what each paint does).

            3D forms

            A cube, sphere, cylinder, cone, pyramid, prism, torus, capsule or egg under a light you control. Click a form to select it, the floor to throw a shadow there, drag to orbit, scroll to zoom; G R S move, rotate and scale. The panel has tabs: Object, Light, View, Use. Open in viewer sends the render to the session viewer; Draw random forms starts a timed session of random scenes.

            Yours to keep

            Everything stays in this browser unless you run it behind the container, which keeps uploads on its disk. Your data (on the dashboard) shows where things are kept and makes a backup of everything. The theme menu has Catppuccin Mocha (the default), Macchiato, Frappé, Latte, Studio dark and Daylight, and a theme editor.
